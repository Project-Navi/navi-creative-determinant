\begin{abstract}
We present a formal mathematical realization of autopoietic closure in the sense of Maturana and Varela~\cite{MaturanaVarela1980}, expressed as a nonlinear elliptic boundary value problem on a compact Riemannian manifold.
The \emph{Creative Determinant} (CD) framework asks: \emph{when can coherent presence sustain itself?}
On a compact Riemannian manifold \(M\) (the \emph{semiotic manifold}), we study the BVP with logistic saturation,
\[
-\Delta \Phi = a(x)\,\abs{\nabla \Phi} + b(x)\,\Phi - c(x)\,\Phi^p\quad\text{in }M,\qquad \Phi=0\quad\text{on }\partial M,
\]
where \(a(x)=\kappa(x)\gamma(x)\mu(x)\in[0,1]\) encodes the \emph{creative drive} (care \(\times\) coherence \(\times\) contradiction), \(b(x)\) is the \emph{viability potential} (autopoietic support minus dissolution, with canonical closure \(b=\kappa\gamma - \lambda\mu\)), and \(c(x)\Phi^p\) enforces \emph{carrying capacity}.
The principal eigenvalue condition \(\lambda_1(-\Delta - b; M) < 0\) provides a computable criterion for the emergence of self-sustaining coherent configurations: it is sufficient in general and exact when the gradient coefficient vanishes---formalizing the transition from incoherence to autopoietic organization.
We set up a Schaefer fixed-point framework with explicit a priori bounds for nonnegative \emph{weak coherent configurations}, and prove by an ordered-barrier argument that when viability exceeds dissipation the BVP admits a solution that is positive throughout the interior, so coherent presence can be self-maintained.
Beyond the PDE core, we distinguish weak from strong coherence (a gap we treat as the framework's central generative tension), formulate conditional geometric statements, and propose an operational \emph{Creative Determinant condition} \(CD(\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta)\) with five explicit falsifiability criteria.
The core definitions, the algebraic and order-theoretic lemmas, and a finite-graph version of the positive-existence theorem with an explicit worked example are machine-checked in Lean~4 against the Mathlib library; the continuum existence theorems are formalized only as implications from an explicit analytic interface whose hypotheses are discharged classically in this paper, not in Lean. Numerical validation confirms second-order grid convergence and reproduces the verified finite-graph example. All proofs, computations, and verification artifacts are open source.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction (interdisciplinary orientation)}
Symbolic and subsymbolic approaches to cognition and computation are often treated as fundamentally distinct: symbols as discrete tokens vs.\ patterns as distributed fields. This paper develops a synthesis in which ``symbolic'' structure is represented as a continuous field over a manifold of meanings. The resulting framework---the \emph{Creative Determinant} (CD)---formalizes the emergence and maintenance of coherent ``presence'' through the interaction of four ingredients:
(i) gradient structure, (ii) care/adaptivity, (iii) coherence/consistency, and (iv) contradiction/curvature.

The manuscript is structured intentionally for an interdisciplinary audience while maintaining a rigorous mathematical spine:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Mathematical core (Sections 2--3).} A nonlinear elliptic PDE with logistic saturation on a compact Riemannian manifold, with a Schaefer/Leray--Schauder framework for nonnegative solutions, a positive-existence theorem using the eigenvalue threshold and ordered barriers, an exact threshold when the gradient term vanishes, and a machine-checked finite-graph analogue.
\item \textbf{Interpretive/structural layer (Section 4).} A careful distinction between weak (PDE) and strong (pointwise) coherence; conditional geometric statements framed as explicit ans\"atze rather than theorems about the PDE.
\item \textbf{Operational layer (Section 5).} A testable dynamical-systems condition and empirical falsification protocols, stated as proposals rather than proved consequences.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{The central generative tension}
A defining feature of this framework is the \emph{necessary gap} between two notions of coherence:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Strong coherence}: the idealized pointwise constraint \(\Phi = |\nabla\Phi|\kappa\gamma\mu\).
\item \textbf{Weak coherence}: solutions to the associated PDE~\eqref{eq:mainBVP}, which are mathematically tractable.
\end{itemize}
This gap is not a limitation---it is the \emph{core philosophical commitment}. Real systems do not achieve idealized coherence; they \emph{strive toward it} while settling into achievable configurations. The distance between strong and weak coherence represents the \emph{work of living}---the ongoing effort required to approach but never fully reach ideal integration. This aligns with enactivist insights~\cite{Thompson2007}: meaning is not a static state but an ongoing process of sense-making in the face of environmental perturbation.

\subsection*{Main mathematical contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \textbf{Existence framework (Theorem~\ref{thm:existenceV1prime}):} Under mild coefficient assumptions, the autonomous viability model with saturation has a compact fixed-point formulation with explicit a priori bounds for nonnegative weak coherent configurations; the zero field is always one of them.
\item \textbf{Positive existence (Theorem~\ref{thm:nontrivialV1prime}):} When the viability threshold is exceeded (\(\lambda_1(-\Delta - b; M) < 0\)), there exists a solution positive throughout the interior, enclosed between a small multiple of the principal eigenfunction and a constant---coherent presence can be self-maintained.
\item \textbf{Exact threshold without drive (Proposition~\ref{prop:exactThreshold}):} When \(a\equiv 0\) the spectral condition is also necessary, so the threshold is exact in that regime; with a gradient term it remains sufficient only.
\item \textbf{Finite-graph theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:graphExistence}):} A finite-graph version of the positive-existence theorem, proved without analytic hypotheses and machine-checked, with an explicit example, a maximum bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:graphBound}), and a counterexample to the converse (Proposition~\ref{prop:graphConverse}).
\item \textbf{Canonical viability closure (Definition~\ref{def:canonicalClosure}):} The viability potential \(b = \kappa\gamma - \lambda\mu\) provides a principled link between the PDE coefficient and the CD characteristic fields.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Main mathematical object and scope}
The primary analytic object is the Dirichlet BVP with logistic saturation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mainBVP}
\begin{cases}
-\Delta \Phi = a(x)\,\abs{\nabla \Phi} + b(x)\,\Phi - c(x)\,\Phi^p & \text{in } M,\\
\Phi = 0 & \text{on } \partial M,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where \(a(x):=\kappa(x)\gamma(x)\mu(x)\in[0,1]\), \(b\in C^{0,\alpha}(M)\), \(c(x)\ge c_0 > 0\), and \(p>1\).
Solutions of \eqref{eq:mainBVP} are called \emph{weak coherent configurations}. The pointwise CD constraint
\(\Phi=\abs{\nabla\Phi}\,\kappa\gamma\mu\) is treated as a constitutive idealization that motivates \eqref{eq:mainBVP}, but is not asserted equivalent; clarifying their relationship is an explicit open problem.

\section{Mathematical setup}
\subsection{Semiotic manifold and function spaces}
\begin{definition}[Semiotic manifold]\label{def:semioticManifold}
Let \((M,g)\) be a compact, connected, smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension \(n\ge 1\) with smooth boundary \(\partial M\). For the Dirichlet problem \eqref{eq:mainBVP} we require \(\partial M\ne\emptyset\); the closed case \(\partial M=\emptyset\) appears only in Proposition~\ref{prop:strongObstruction}.
We denote by \(\nabla\) the Riemannian gradient and by \(\Delta\) the Laplace--Beltrami operator.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Terminology: ``semiotic'']
The term \emph{semiotic manifold} reflects the framework's roots in Peircean semiotics~\cite{Peirce1931}, where meaning emerges through sign relations. The manifold \(M\) is interpreted as a space of possible meanings or interpretations.
\end{remark}

We use standard H\"older spaces \(C^{k,\alpha}(\overline M)\) for \(k\in\mathbb{N}\), \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and Sobolev spaces \(W^{k,q}(M)\), with the usual norms; see \cite{GilbargTrudinger2001,Evans2010}.
The fixed-point arguments of Section~\ref{sec:mainPDE} take place in
\[
X := \{\psi\in C^1(\overline M):\ \psi|_{\partial M}=0\},
\]
and we interpret \(\abs{\nabla \psi}\) pointwise using the Riemannian norm induced by \(g\).

\subsection{Core scalar fields and the coefficient \(a\)}
\begin{definition}[Characteristic fields]\label{def:fields}
Let \(\kappa,\gamma,\mu\in C^{0,\alpha}(M)\) be scalar fields with values in \([0,1]\). Define
\[
a(x) := \kappa(x)\gamma(x)\mu(x)\in[0,1].
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Primary boundary condition]
Dirichlet boundary conditions are taken as primary for analytic clarity and interpretive meaning: \(\Phi=0\) on \(\partial M\) represents boundary contexts where sustained presence/coherence is absent.
Neumann conditions are discussed only as a secondary variant.
\end{remark}

\section{Main PDE Results}\label{sec:mainPDE}

\noindent\textbf{Guiding intuition.}
Before the formal machinery, here is the central question: \emph{when can coherent presence sustain itself?}
The answer involves a spectral condition---when the viability potential \(b(x) = \kappa\gamma - \lambda\mu\) (care-coherence support minus contradiction cost) is strong enough to overcome boundary dissipation. This is made precise by the condition \(\lambda_1(-\Delta - b; M) < 0\), which we call the \emph{viability threshold}. The theorems below establish that crossing this threshold guarantees the existence of non-trivial coherent configurations. The proofs use classical fixed-point machinery, but the conceptual payoff is immediate: \emph{viability exceeds dissipation} $\Rightarrow$ \emph{coherent presence can be self-maintained}.

\subsection{Primary model: autonomous viability with logistic saturation (V1')}
Let
\[
a(x) := \kappa(x)\gamma(x)\mu(x)\in[0,1]
\]
be the CD modulation coefficient. We introduce a \emph{viability potential} \(b\) and a \emph{saturation (carrying capacity) field} \(c\) to obtain a mathematically nondegenerate autonomous equilibrium model.

\begin{definition}[Autonomous viability model with saturation (V1')]\label{def:V1prime}
Fix \(p>1\). Given coefficients
\[
a\in C^{0,\alpha}(M),\quad 0\le a\le 1;\qquad
b\in C^{0,\alpha}(M);\qquad
c\in C^{0,\alpha}(M),\quad c(x)\ge c_0>0,
\]
we consider the Dirichlet problem
\begin{equation}\label{eq:V1prime}
\begin{cases}
-\Delta \Phi = a(x)\,|\nabla \Phi| + b(x)\,\Phi - c(x)\,\Phi^{p} & \text{in }M,\\
\Phi=0 & \text{on }\partial M.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
A solution \(\Phi\in C^{2,\alpha}(M)\) of \eqref{eq:V1prime} is called a \emph{weak coherent configuration} for the autonomous CD model.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Interpretive glossary for \eqref{eq:V1prime}]
In \eqref{eq:V1prime}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(a(x)=\kappa\gamma\mu\) is the \emph{creative drive} term: gradient activity contributes to presence only where care, coherence, and contradiction jointly support it.
\item \(b(x)>0\) is \emph{viability gain} / autopoietic support: presence amplifies where sustainable.
\item \(b(x)<0\) is a \emph{dissolution zone}: presence decays locally.
\item \(c(x)\Phi^p\) enforces \emph{carrying capacity} / resource limitation: it prevents unbounded growth and encodes finite-resource autopoiesis.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Canonical viability closure]\label{def:canonicalClosure}
Under the \emph{canonical viability closure}, the viability potential is defined by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonicalb}
b(x) := \kappa(x)\gamma(x) - \lambda\,\mu(x),
\end{equation}
where \(\lambda>0\) is a contradiction-weighting parameter.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Empirical motivation for the linear closure]
The canonical closure \(b = \kappa\gamma - \lambda\mu\) is not derived from first principles, but it is not arbitrary either. It represents the \emph{simplest mass-action balance} reflecting a fundamental trade-off observed across biological, psychological, and social systems: \textbf{support} (care \(\times\) coherence) competes against \textbf{contradiction cost} (\(\lambda \times\) contradiction level).

This structure echoes the ``recovery capital'' framework in behavioral health~\cite{White2008,Granfield1999}: sustained wellbeing emerges when supportive resources outweigh risk factors. The linear form is the minimal assumption---the V1 approximation---before introducing higher-order effects. Alternative closures (multiplicative, exponential, threshold-based) remain open for investigation, but the linear closure captures the essential bifurcation structure: when support dominates cost, nontrivial configurations exist; when cost dominates, the system collapses to triviality.

Seven years of observation in peer support and recovery research informed this choice: systems that sustain coherence reliably exhibit this support-versus-cost dynamic. The math formalizes what experience revealed.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The contradiction-cost parameter \(\lambda\)]\label{rem:lambdaCost}
The parameter \(\lambda\) is not merely a tuning knob---it is an \emph{empirically observable characteristic} of a system's sensitivity to internal contradiction. Crucially, in the closure \eqref{eq:canonicalb}, \(\lambda\) functions as a \textbf{cost multiplier}: increasing \(\lambda\) decreases \(b\) for fixed \(\mu>0\), making the spectral condition \(\lambda_1(-\Delta-b;M)<0\) harder to satisfy.
\begin{itemize}
\item A system with \textbf{high \(\lambda\)} (contradiction-sensitive) experiences contradiction as costly: even moderate \(\mu\) significantly reduces viability. Example: a brittle belief system that fragments when faced with counter-evidence.
\item A system with \textbf{low \(\lambda\)} (contradiction-resilient) can sustain coherence even when \(\mu\) is elevated, because contradiction exerts less drag on viability. Example: a healthy cognitive system that integrates conflicting information without destabilizing.
\end{itemize}
This interpretation provides a concrete empirical program: estimate \(\lambda\) from observed bifurcation points where systems transition from coherent to incoherent behavior. Systems at the ``cliff edge'' of viability (small negative \(\lambda_1\)) are those where care-coherence support barely exceeds contradiction cost---a measurable condition of precarious stability.

We emphasize that the closure parameter \(\lambda\) in \eqref{eq:canonicalb} is distinct from the principal eigenvalue \(\lambda_1(-\Delta-b;M)\): the former weights contradiction in the viability potential, while the latter is a spectral indicator of whether nontrivial coherence can exist (Theorem~\ref{thm:nontrivialV1prime}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Limiting case: the homogeneous model (V1)]
If \(c\equiv 0\), \eqref{eq:V1prime} reduces to the positively homogeneous equation
\[
-\Delta\Phi = a(x)|\nabla\Phi| + b(x)\Phi,\qquad \Phi|_{\partial M}=0,
\]
which is an idealized ``no-saturation'' limit. The saturation term is mathematically and interpretively important: it breaks homogeneity and enables barrier constructions yielding nontrivial equilibria.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Existence of nonnegative weak coherent configurations (Schaefer/Leray--Schauder)}\label{sec:existence}
We now set up the fixed-point framework for \eqref{eq:V1prime}. Throughout, \(\partial M\neq\emptyset\) (the Dirichlet problem needs a boundary), \(n=\dim M\), and \(q>n\) is a fixed exponent. All estimates cited from Gilbarg--Trudinger~\cite{GilbargTrudinger2001} are stated there for bounded domains in \(\R^n\) with smooth boundary; on a compact manifold with smooth boundary they hold for the Laplace--Beltrami operator, which is uniformly elliptic with smooth coefficients in every chart, by localization and a partition of unity, and we use them in that form.

\paragraph{What changed.}
An earlier version of this section worked with the operator \(\widetilde T(\psi)=S\big(a|\nabla\psi|+b\psi-c\,(\psi_+)^p\big)\) in H\"older spaces and asserted a uniform bound \(\|u\|_{L^\infty}\le K\) for its Schaefer set together with nonnegativity of its fixed points. Both assertions are false: on \(M=(0,\pi)\) with \(a=0\), \(b=c=1\), \(p=2\), every \(u_A(x)=-A\sin x\), \(A>0\), satisfies \(-u_A''=u_A=u_A-(u_A)_+^2\), hence \(\widetilde T(u_A)=u_A\), so \(\widetilde T\) has negative fixed points of arbitrarily large norm. The repair below truncates the linear term as well, works in \(C^1\) rather than \(C^{1,\alpha}\) (the map \(\psi\mapsto|\nabla\psi|\) is bounded but not continuous in H\"older norm), and obtains nonnegativity from the weak maximum principle before the \(L^\infty\) bound.

\subsubsection*{Operator formulation}
Let
\[
X:=\{\psi\in C^1(\overline M):\ \psi|_{\partial M}=0\},\qquad \|\psi\|_X:=\|\psi\|_{C^1(\overline M)},
\]
a Banach space. For \(\psi\in X\) define the \emph{truncated nonlinearity}
\[
F(\psi)(x):=a(x)\,|\nabla\psi(x)|+b(x)\,\psi_+(x)-c(x)\,\big(\psi_+(x)\big)^p,\qquad \psi_+:=\max\{\psi,0\}.
\]
For \(\psi\ge 0\) this is the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:V1prime}. Let \(S\) denote the Dirichlet solution operator of \(-\Delta\): for \(f\in L^q(M)\) there is a unique \(u=Sf\in W^{2,q}(M)\cap W^{1,q}_0(M)\) with \(-\Delta u=f\), and \(\|Sf\|_{W^{2,q}}\le C\|f\|_{L^q}\)~\cite[Thm.~9.15, Lem.~9.17]{GilbargTrudinger2001}. Define
\[
T:X\to X,\qquad T(\psi):=S\big(F(\psi)\big).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Compactness]\label{lem:TcompactV1prime}
\(T:X\to X\) is well defined, continuous, and compact.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The maps \(\xi\mapsto|\xi|\) on \(\R^n\), \(s\mapsto s_+\) and \(s\mapsto (s_+)^p\) on \(\R\) are continuous, so \(\psi\mapsto F(\psi)\) is continuous from \(X\) to \(C^0(\overline M)\) (uniformly on bounded sets) and bounded: \(\|F(\psi)\|_{\infty}\le\|\nabla\psi\|_\infty+\|b\|_\infty\|\psi\|_\infty+\|c\|_\infty\|\psi\|_\infty^p\). Composing with the bounded operator \(S:L^q\to W^{2,q}\) and the Sobolev embedding \(W^{2,q}(M)\hookrightarrow C^{1,\gamma}(\overline M)\), \(\gamma=1-n/q\)~\cite[Thm.~7.26]{GilbargTrudinger2001}, shows that \(T\) is continuous into \(C^{1,\gamma}(\overline M)\) and maps bounded sets of \(X\) to bounded sets of \(C^{1,\gamma}(\overline M)\). Bounded sets of \(C^{1,\gamma}(\overline M)\) are relatively compact in \(C^1(\overline M)\) (Arzel\`a--Ascoli), and \(T(\psi)\) vanishes on \(\partial M\). Hence \(T:X\to X\) is continuous and compact.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{A priori bounds for Schaefer}
Schaefer's theorem (\cite[\S9.2.2, Thm.~4]{Evans2010}; \cite{Schaefer1955,Deimling1985}) gives a fixed point of a continuous compact self-map of a Banach space once the set
\[
\mathcal S:=\{u\in X:\ u=\tau T(u)\ \text{for some }\tau\in[0,1]\}
\]
is bounded. Elements of \(\mathcal S\) satisfy \(u\in W^{2,q}(M)\) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schaeferEq}
-\Delta u=\tau F(u)=\tau\Big(a|\nabla u|+b\,u_+-c\,(u_+)^p\Big)\ \text{a.e. in }M,\qquad u|_{\partial M}=0 .
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Upper bound on \(b\)]\label{assump:bupper}
Let \(B\ge 0\) satisfy \(b(x)\le B\) for all \(x\in M\). Since \(b\in C^{0,\alpha}(M)\) and \(M\) is compact, \(B:=\max\{\max_M b,\,0\}\) always works; the assumption only names the constant that enters the bounds below.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Regularity and sign of the Schaefer set]\label{lem:schaeferSign}
Every \(u\in\mathcal S\) belongs to \(C^{2,\gamma'}(\overline M)\) with \(\gamma':=\min\{\alpha,\gamma\}\) and satisfies \(u\ge 0\) on \(M\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(u=\tau T(u)\). By the Sobolev embedding \(u\in C^{1,\gamma}(\overline M)\), so \(|\nabla u|\), \(u_+\) and \((u_+)^p\) are in \(C^{0,\gamma}(\overline M)\) (compositions with the Lipschitz maps \(|\cdot|\), \((\cdot)_+\), and the locally Lipschitz map \(s\mapsto (s_+)^p\), \(p>1\)). Thus \(\tau F(u)\in C^{0,\gamma'}(\overline M)\), and the Dirichlet problem \(-\Delta v=\tau F(u)\), \(v|_{\partial M}=0\), has a solution \(v\in C^{2,\gamma'}(\overline M)\)~\cite[Thm.~6.14]{GilbargTrudinger2001}; by uniqueness in \(W^{2,q}\cap W^{1,q}_0\), \(v=u\).

For the sign, let \(U:=\{x\in M^\circ:\ u(x)<0\}\), an open set, and suppose \(U\ne\emptyset\). On \(U\), \(u_+=0\), so \(-\Delta u=\tau a|\nabla u|\ge 0\) pointwise. Put \(\beta(x):=\tau a(x)\nabla u(x)/|\nabla u(x)|\) where \(\nabla u(x)\ne 0\) and \(\beta(x):=0\) otherwise; \(\beta\) is bounded and measurable with \(|\beta|\le 1\), and \(\tau a|\nabla u|=\beta\cdot\nabla u\). Then \(w:=-u\in C^2(U)\cap C^0(\overline U)\) satisfies \(\Delta w+\beta\cdot\nabla w=0\) in \(U\), \(w>0\) in \(U\), and \(w=0\) on \(\partial U\) (on \(\partial M\) because \(u|_{\partial M}=0\), and elsewhere on \(\partial U\) by continuity). The weak maximum principle for elliptic operators with bounded coefficients~\cite[Thm.~3.1]{GilbargTrudinger2001} gives \(\sup_U w=\sup_{\partial U}w=0\), a contradiction. Hence \(U=\emptyset\) and \(u\ge 0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform \(L^\infty\) bound]\label{lem:LinftyBound}
Under Assumption~\ref{assump:bupper}, every \(u\in\mathcal S\) satisfies
\[
0\le u\le K:=\Big(\frac{B}{c_0}\Big)^{1/(p-1)}\quad\text{on }M .
\]
More generally, every nonnegative solution \(\Phi\in C^2(\overline M)\) of \eqref{eq:V1prime} satisfies \(\max_M\Phi\le K\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:schaeferSign}, \(u\ge 0\) and \(u\) is a classical solution of \eqref{eq:schaeferEq}, in which now \(u_+=u\). If \(\max_M u=0\) there is nothing to prove, and if \(\tau=0\) then \(u=0\). Otherwise the maximum is attained at \(x_0\in M^\circ\) (since \(u=0\) on \(\partial M\)), where \(\nabla u(x_0)=0\) and \(-\Delta u(x_0)\ge 0\). Evaluating \eqref{eq:schaeferEq} at \(x_0\),
\[
0\le \tau\big(b(x_0)u(x_0)-c(x_0)u(x_0)^p\big)\le\tau\big(B\,u(x_0)-c_0\,u(x_0)^p\big),
\]
and since \(\tau>0\) and \(u(x_0)>0\), this gives \(u(x_0)^{p-1}\le B/c_0\). The same evaluation with \(\tau=1\) proves the statement for nonnegative classical solutions. (The algebraic step from \(Bv\ge c_0v^p\) to \(v\le (B/c_0)^{1/(p-1)}\) is the Lean lemma \texttt{linfty\_bound\_algebraic}; see Appendix~\ref{app:verification}.)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform \(C^1\) bound]\label{lem:C1alphaBound}
Under Assumption~\ref{assump:bupper}, \(\mathcal S\) is bounded in \(X\); in fact it is bounded in \(C^{1,\gamma}(\overline M)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(u\in\mathcal S\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:LinftyBound}, \(|\tau F(u)|\le |\nabla u|+C_0\) pointwise, with \(C_0:=BK+\|c\|_\infty K^p\). The \(W^{2,q}\) estimate for the Dirichlet problem~\cite[Lem.~9.17]{GilbargTrudinger2001} and the interpolation inequality~\cite[Thm.~7.28]{GilbargTrudinger2001} give, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\|u\|_{W^{2,q}}\le C\|\tau F(u)\|_{L^q}\le C\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^q}+C_0|M|^{1/q}\big)
\le C\big(\varepsilon\|u\|_{W^{2,q}}+C_\varepsilon\|u\|_{L^q}+C_0|M|^{1/q}\big).
\]
Choosing \(\varepsilon=1/(2C)\) and using \(\|u\|_{L^q}\le K|M|^{1/q}\) yields \(\|u\|_{W^{2,q}}\le C'\), with \(C'\) depending only on \(M,q,B,c_0,p\) and \(\|c\|_\infty\). The Sobolev embedding then bounds \(\|u\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\), hence \(\|u\|_X\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Existence of a nonnegative weak coherent configuration]\label{thm:existenceV1prime}
Assume the coefficient hypotheses of Definition~\ref{def:V1prime}. Then \(T\) has a fixed point \(\Phi\in X\); every fixed point of \(T\) is a nonnegative solution \(\Phi\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline M)\) of \eqref{eq:V1prime}, and every nonnegative classical solution satisfies \(\max_M\Phi\le (B/c_0)^{1/(p-1)}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Lemmas~\ref{lem:TcompactV1prime} and~\ref{lem:C1alphaBound}, Schaefer's theorem yields \(\Phi\in X\) with \(T(\Phi)=\Phi\), i.e.\ \(\Phi\in\mathcal S\) with \(\tau=1\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:schaeferSign}, \(\Phi\ge 0\) and \(\Phi\in C^{2,\gamma'}(\overline M)\); since \(\Phi_+=\Phi\), \(\Phi\) solves \eqref{eq:V1prime}. One more Schauder step upgrades the regularity: \(\nabla\Phi\in C^{1,\gamma'}\) makes \(|\nabla\Phi|\) Lipschitz, so the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:V1prime} lies in \(C^{0,\alpha}(\overline M)\) and \(\Phi\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline M)\)~\cite[Thm.~6.14]{GilbargTrudinger2001}. The bound is Lemma~\ref{lem:LinftyBound}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The zero solution.}
\(\Phi\equiv 0\) solves \eqref{eq:V1prime} for every admissible choice of coefficients (Lean: \texttt{zero\_solves\_equation}), so Theorem~\ref{thm:existenceV1prime} does not by itself produce coherent presence. Its content is the fixed-point framework and the a priori bounds, which Theorem~\ref{thm:nontrivialV1prime} reuses; the substantive question is the existence of a solution that is positive in the interior.

\subsection{Viability exceeds dissipation: the condition for non-trivial coherence}\label{sec:positive}
The existence theorem above guarantees a nonnegative solution, and the zero function is one. The following theorem provides the key criterion: when does the system support \emph{genuine} coherent presence? The answer is a spectral condition on the viability potential---precisely, when integrated care-coherence support dominates contradiction load strongly enough to overcome boundary dissipation.

\begin{definition}[Principal eigenvalue and viability threshold]\label{def:lambda1}
Let \(\lambda_1(-\Delta - b;M)\) denote the principal Dirichlet eigenvalue of the operator
\(\mathcal{L}_b u := -\Delta u - b(x)u\) on \(M\).
We say the \emph{viability threshold is exceeded} if
\[
\lambda_1(-\Delta - b;M) < 0.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Spectral theory]
For the symmetric operator \(\mathcal L_b\) with Dirichlet conditions, \(\lambda_1\) is simple and has an eigenfunction \(\phi_1\) that is positive in \(M^\circ\); see~\cite[\S6.5.2]{Evans2010} and, for the general nonsymmetric setting, the Krein--Rutman theorem~\cite{KreinRutman1948}. Since \(b\in C^{0,\alpha}(M)\), Schauder regularity gives \(\phi_1\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline M)\).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation of the viability threshold]\label{rem:viabilityInterpretation}
Under the canonical closure \(b=\kappa\gamma-\lambda\mu\) (Definition~\ref{def:canonicalClosure}), the spectral condition
\(\lambda_1(-\Delta - b;M)<0\) becomes a precise \emph{``viability exceeds dissipation''} criterion:
integrated care--coherence support dominates contradiction load strongly enough to overcome boundary dissipation imposed by \(\Phi|_{\partial M}=0\).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Existence of a positive weak coherent configuration]\label{thm:nontrivialV1prime}
Assume the coefficient hypotheses of Definition~\ref{def:V1prime} and suppose
\[
\lambda_1(-\Delta - b;M) < 0.
\]
Let \(\phi_1\) be the principal eigenfunction normalized by \(\|\phi_1\|_{L^\infty}=1\), and set
\[
\varepsilon:=\min\Big\{1,\Big(\frac{-\lambda_1}{\|c\|_\infty}\Big)^{1/(p-1)}\Big\},\qquad
M_\ast:=\max\Big\{1,\Big(\frac{B}{c_0}\Big)^{1/(p-1)}\Big\}.
\]
Then \eqref{eq:V1prime} admits a solution \(\Phi\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline M)\) with
\[
\varepsilon\,\phi_1\le\Phi\le M_\ast\quad\text{in }M;\qquad\text{in particular }\Phi>0\text{ in }M^\circ .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(g(x,s):=b(x)s-c(x)s^p\) for \(s\ge 0\) and \(\beta:=-\lambda_1>0\), so that \(-\Delta\phi_1=b\phi_1-\beta\phi_1\).

\paragraph{Step 1: ordered barriers.}
\emph{Subsolution.} Let \(v:=\varepsilon\phi_1\). Then \(v=0\) on \(\partial M\), \(v>0\) in \(M^\circ\), and
\[
-\Delta v=\varepsilon b\phi_1-\varepsilon\beta\phi_1 .
\]
The subsolution inequality \(-\Delta v\le a|\nabla v|+g(x,v)\) holds on \(\partial M\) with equality (both sides vanish) and in \(M^\circ\) reads, after cancelling \(\varepsilon b\phi_1\) and dividing by \(\varepsilon\phi_1>0\),
\[
-\beta\le a\,\frac{|\nabla\phi_1|}{\phi_1}-c\,\varepsilon^{p-1}\phi_1^{p-1}.
\]
Since \(a|\nabla\phi_1|\ge 0\) and \(0<\phi_1\le 1\), it suffices that \(\|c\|_\infty\varepsilon^{p-1}\le\beta\), which is the choice of \(\varepsilon\).

\emph{Supersolution.} The constant \(w:=M_\ast\) satisfies \(w\ge 0=\Phi|_{\partial M}\) on \(\partial M\) and \(-\Delta w=0\), \(|\nabla w|=0\), so \(-\Delta w\ge a|\nabla w|+g(x,w)\) is equivalent to \(c(x)M_\ast^{p-1}\ge b(x)\), which follows from \(c_0M_\ast^{p-1}\ge B\). A supersolution need only dominate the zero boundary data; it need not vanish on \(\partial M\).

\emph{Ordering.} \(v\le\varepsilon\le 1\le M_\ast=w\).

\paragraph{Step 2: a truncated problem.}
Let \(\theta(x,s):=\min\{\max\{s,v(x)\},M_\ast\}\in[v(x),M_\ast]\) and choose
\[
K:=1+\max\Big\{0,\ \|c\|_\infty\,p\,M_\ast^{p-1}-\min_M b\Big\},
\]
so that \(s\mapsto g(x,s)+Ks\) is nondecreasing on \([0,M_\ast]\) for every \(x\) (its derivative is \(b-c\,p\,s^{p-1}+K\ge 1\)). Consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truncated}
-\Delta u+Ku=a|\nabla u|+h(x,u)\ \text{in }M,\qquad u|_{\partial M}=0,\qquad h(x,s):=g\big(x,\theta(x,s)\big)+K\theta(x,s).
\end{equation}
The function \(h\) is continuous and bounded, \(|h|\le H:=BM_\ast+\|c\|_\infty M_\ast^p+KM_\ast\). Let \(S_K\) be the Dirichlet solution operator of \(-\Delta+K\), bounded \(L^q\to W^{2,q}\cap W^{1,q}_0\) with \(\|S_Kf\|_{W^{2,q}}\le C\|f\|_{L^q}\)~\cite[Thm.~9.15, Lem.~9.17]{GilbargTrudinger2001} (the zero-order coefficient \(-K\) is nonpositive), and define \(T_K(\psi):=S_K\big(a|\nabla\psi|+h(\cdot,\psi)\big)\) on \(X\). Exactly as in Lemma~\ref{lem:TcompactV1prime}, \(T_K:X\to X\) is continuous and compact.

Let \(u=\tau T_K(u)\), \(\tau\in[0,1]\). As in Lemma~\ref{lem:schaeferSign}, \(u\in C^{1,\gamma}(\overline M)\); moreover \(\theta(\cdot,u(\cdot))\) is a minimum and maximum of H\"older functions, hence H\"older, and \(g\) is \(C^1\) in \(s\) with H\"older coefficients, so \(h(\cdot,u(\cdot))\in C^{0,\gamma'}(\overline M)\) and \(u\in C^{2,\gamma'}(\overline M)\) is a classical solution of \(-\Delta u+Ku=\tau(a|\nabla u|+h(x,u))\). At a positive interior maximum \(x_0\) of \(u\), \(\nabla u(x_0)=0\) and \(-\Delta u(x_0)\ge 0\), whence \(Ku(x_0)\le\tau H\le H\); at a negative interior minimum, \(Ku\ge -H\). Hence \(\|u\|_\infty\le H/K\), and the argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:C1alphaBound} (with \(|\tau(a|\nabla u|+h)|\le|\nabla u|+H\)) bounds the Schaefer set of \(T_K\) in \(X\). Schaefer's theorem gives a fixed point \(u\in X\) of \(T_K\), which is a classical solution of \eqref{eq:truncated} in \(C^{2,\gamma'}(\overline M)\).

\paragraph{Step 3: comparison with the barriers.}
Set \(U:=\{x\in M^\circ:\ u(x)<v(x)\}\), open, and suppose \(U\ne\emptyset\). On \(U\) we have \(u<v\le M_\ast\), hence \(\theta(x,u)=v(x)\) and \(h(x,u)=g(x,v)+Kv\). Using the subsolution inequality for \(v\), the difference \(z:=v-u\in C^2(U)\cap C^0(\overline U)\) satisfies in \(U\)
\[
-\Delta z+Kz\le a|\nabla v|+g(x,v)+Kv-\big(a|\nabla u|+g(x,v)+Kv\big)=a\big(|\nabla v|-|\nabla u|\big)\le a|\nabla z| .
\]
With \(\beta:=a\nabla z/|\nabla z|\) where \(\nabla z\ne 0\) (and \(0\) elsewhere), this is \(\Delta z+\beta\cdot\nabla z-Kz\ge 0\) in \(U\), an inequality for a uniformly elliptic operator with bounded coefficients and nonpositive zero-order term. Since \(z>0\) in \(U\) and \(z=0\) on \(\partial U\) (\(u=v=0\) on \(\partial M\); \(z=0\) on the interior part of \(\partial U\) by continuity), the weak maximum principle~\cite[Cor.~3.2]{GilbargTrudinger2001} gives \(\sup_U z\le\sup_{\partial U}z^+=0\), a contradiction. Thus \(u\ge v\).

Similarly let \(U':=\{x\in M^\circ:\ u(x)>M_\ast\}\); on \(\partial M\), \(u=0<M_\ast\), so \(\overline{U'}\cap\partial M=\emptyset\). On \(U'\), \(\theta(x,u)=M_\ast\), so \(z:=u-M_\ast\) satisfies
\[
-\Delta z+Kz=a|\nabla u|+g(x,M_\ast)+KM_\ast-KM_\ast=a|\nabla z|+g(x,M_\ast)\le a|\nabla z|,
\]
because \(g(x,M_\ast)=M_\ast\big(b(x)-c(x)M_\ast^{p-1}\big)\le 0\). The same maximum principle on \(U'\), where \(z>0\) and \(z=0\) on \(\partial U'\), forces \(U'=\emptyset\). Thus \(u\le M_\ast\).

\paragraph{Step 4: conclusion.}
Since \(v\le u\le M_\ast\), \(\theta(x,u(x))=u(x)\) and \eqref{eq:truncated} reduces to \(-\Delta u+Ku=a|\nabla u|+g(x,u)+Ku\), i.e.\ \(u\) solves \eqref{eq:V1prime}. The Schauder step of Theorem~\ref{thm:existenceV1prime} gives \(u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline M)\), and \(u\ge\varepsilon\phi_1>0\) in \(M^\circ\). Set \(\Phi:=u\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What changed, and the relation to the sub-/super-solution literature.}
The earlier version of this proof used the super-solution \(K\phi_1^2\). That construction fails for two independent reasons. First, \(-\Delta(\phi_1^2)=2\phi_1(-\Delta\phi_1)-2|\nabla\phi_1|^2\): the gradient term enters with a negative sign, which reverses the boundary behaviour claimed there (for \(\phi_1=\sin x\) on \((0,\pi)\), \(-\Delta(\phi_1^2)=-2\cos 2x<0\) near the boundary). Second, no constant \(K\) makes \(K\phi_1^2\ge\varepsilon\phi_1\) hold up to a Dirichlet boundary: the inequality requires \(K\phi_1\ge\varepsilon\), which fails wherever \(0<\phi_1<\varepsilon/K\). A constant super-solution has neither problem. The barrier pair \((\varepsilon\phi_1,M_\ast)\) is the classical one for problems with gradient dependence of at most quadratic growth, going back to Amann and Crandall~\cite{AmannCrandall1978} and the survey~\cite{Amann1976}; Steps~2--3 above give a self-contained proof in the present setting rather than an appeal to a general theorem, so that every hypothesis used is visible.

\subsection{Master equation and the driven/coupled case}
\begin{remark}[Master equation and specializations]\label{rem:masterEquation}
For generality one may consider the master form
\[
-\Delta\Phi = a(x)|\nabla\Phi| + b(x)\Phi - c(x)\Phi^p + f(x),
\qquad \Phi|_{\partial M}=0,
\]
where \(f\) encodes exogenous significance / environmental input.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Autonomous (closed) case:} \(f\equiv 0\), yielding \eqref{eq:V1prime}.
\item \textbf{Driven (coupled) case:} \(b\equiv 0\), \(c\equiv 0\), yielding a forced, interaction-sustained presence model.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Forcing as the cost of striving]
The exogenous forcing function \(f(x)\) admits a novel interpretation connecting the master equation to the weak/strong coherence distinction. Suppose \(\Phi_{\mathrm{weak}}\) is a stable weak coherent configuration (a solution of the autonomous PDE) and \(\Phi_{\mathrm{strong}}\) is the idealized strong coherence target. Then \(f(x)\) may be modeled as the \emph{measurable residual}---the energy required to pull the system from its stable weak configuration toward the strong coherence ideal.

If the gap between strong and weak coherence is large, the required \(f(x)\) is large; if they nearly coincide, \(f(x)\) is negligible. This reframing provides a roadmap for empirical testing: measure the forcing required to achieve near-ideal coherence, and this quantifies the ``cost of striving.'' The master equation thus bridges the conceptual distinction (Section~4.1) with a measurable dynamical quantity. The word ``energy'' is interpretive here: \(f\) is a forcing term, not a quantity derived from a variational functional, and no such structure is asserted for \eqref{eq:V1prime}. Proposition~\ref{prop:strongObstruction} shows that on a compact manifold with zero boundary data the strong ideal is not attained by any nonnegative \(C^1\) field, so the gap it measures is never zero in that setting.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Exactness of the threshold, the effective potential, and what remains open}\label{sec:exactness}

\begin{proposition}[Exact threshold without a gradient term]\label{prop:exactThreshold}
Assume the coefficient hypotheses of Definition~\ref{def:V1prime} with \(a\equiv 0\). Then \eqref{eq:V1prime} has a solution that is positive in \(M^\circ\) if and only if \(\lambda_1(-\Delta-b;M)<0\). More precisely, if \(\Phi\ge 0\), \(\Phi\not\equiv 0\), is a classical solution, then \(\lambda_1<0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Sufficiency is Theorem~\ref{thm:nontrivialV1prime}. For necessity let \(\Phi\in C^2(\overline M)\), \(\Phi\ge 0\), \(\Phi\not\equiv 0\), solve \(-\Delta\Phi=b\Phi-c\Phi^p\), \(\Phi|_{\partial M}=0\), and let \(\phi_1>0\) in \(M^\circ\) be the principal eigenfunction. Green's second identity (both functions vanish on \(\partial M\)) gives
\[
\int_M(b\Phi-c\Phi^p)\phi_1=\int_M(-\Delta\Phi)\phi_1=\int_M\Phi(-\Delta\phi_1)=\int_M\Phi(b+\lambda_1)\phi_1,
\]
hence
\[
\lambda_1\int_M\Phi\phi_1=-\int_M c\,\Phi^p\phi_1<0 ,
\]
because \(c\ge c_0>0\), \(\phi_1>0\) in \(M^\circ\) and \(\Phi^p\not\equiv 0\). Since \(\int_M\Phi\phi_1>0\), \(\lambda_1<0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The gradient term breaks the converse]\label{rem:converseGradient}
With \(a\not\equiv 0\) the identity becomes \(\lambda_1\int\Phi\phi_1=\int a|\nabla\Phi|\phi_1-\int c\Phi^p\phi_1\), whose sign is not determined, so the argument does not extend. Whether a positive solution of the continuum problem can exist with \(\lambda_1\ge 0\) when \(a>0\) is open. In the finite-graph model of Section~\ref{sec:graph} the converse is false: Proposition~\ref{prop:graphConverse} gives a solution positive at every interior vertex with a positive principal eigenvalue. The spectral condition is therefore exact in the regime \(a\equiv 0\) and a sufficient condition in general; the linear indicator \(\lambda_1\) must not be described as an exact nonlinear threshold outside that regime.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Effective potential and the gain \(\beta\)]\label{rem:effectivePotential}
The numerical companion writes the reaction coefficient as \(q(x):=\beta\,b_{\mathrm{base}}(x)\) with a dimensionless gain \(\beta>0\), because \(\kappa,\gamma,\mu\in[0,1]\) while Dirichlet eigenvalues scale like \(L^{-2}\). All statements in this section apply verbatim with \(b:=q\): the threshold is \(\lambda_1(-\Delta-q;M)<0\), the bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:LinftyBound} uses \(\max q_+\), and \(\beta=1\) recovers the base equation. What is \emph{not} true is that negativity of \(\lambda_1(-\Delta-\beta b;M)\) for some auxiliary \(\beta\) says anything about the equation with coefficient \(b\): on \((0,\pi)\) with \(a=0\), \(c=1\), \(p=2\), \(b=\tfrac12\) and \(\beta=3\), one has \(\lambda_1(-\Delta-\beta b)=-\tfrac12<0\) while \(\lambda_1(-\Delta-b)=\tfrac12>0\), so by Proposition~\ref{prop:exactThreshold} the unscaled equation has no positive solution. The formal interface discussed in Appendix~\ref{app:verification} quantifies over such auxiliary \(\beta\); the correct instantiation is \(b:=q\) with \(\beta=1\).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Uniqueness and stability]\label{rem:uniquenessStatus}
The scaling argument formalized in Lean (\path{scaling_uniqueness}) shows that a solution positive at a point where \(c>0\) has no solution multiple \(k\Phi\), \(k>1\); it says nothing about uniqueness among all positive solutions, which remains open (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). Likewise, an equilibrium solver classifies solutions of the stationary problem; it does not establish dynamical stability or a bifurcation type, for which no evolution law has been specified here.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The finite-graph model (machine-checked)}\label{sec:graph}
The continuum theorems above rest on classical elliptic theory. The Lean~4 development (Appendix~\ref{app:verification}) contains a complete, unconditional proof of the analogous positive-existence theorem for a finite-graph version of \eqref{eq:V1prime}, in which every operator is defined from data. We state that model and theorem here, together with an explicit example, a maximum bound proved for it, and a counterexample to the converse. The finite-graph formulation and the proof route through finite-dimensional inverse positivity, positive principal eigendata and the order-theoretic fixed-point core were proposed by Andrew Edmark; the particular discretization, the Jacobi splitting and the edge condition below are choices of the formalization.

\begin{definition}[Finite-graph CD problem]\label{def:graphModel}
Let \(V\) be a finite set of vertices, \(w:V\times V\to[0,\infty)\) symmetric weights (self weights allowed), \(B\subset V\) a boundary set with interior \(I:=V\setminus B\), and coefficients \(\kappa,\gamma,\mu:V\to[0,1]\), \(a:=\kappa\gamma\mu\), \(b:V\to\R\), \(c:V\to(0,\infty)\), \(p>1\). Define
\[
(L_Gu)(x):=\sum_{y\in V}w(x,y)\big(u(x)-u(y)\big),\qquad
|\nabla_G u|(x):=\Big(\sum_{y\in V}w(x,y)\big(u(y)-u(x)\big)^2\Big)^{1/2}.
\]
A function \(u:V\to\R\) \emph{solves the graph problem} if
\[
L_Gu(x)=a(x)|\nabla_Gu|(x)+b(x)u(x)-c(x)\max\{u(x),0\}^p\quad(x\in I),\qquad u(x)=0\quad(x\in B).
\]
Both sums run over all of \(V\), so edges to \(B\) contribute through the zero boundary values; there is no vertex measure and no degree normalization. The \emph{energy} is \(E(u):=\tfrac12\sum_{x,y}w(x,y)(u(x)-u(y))^2-\sum_xb(x)u(x)^2\), and the \emph{principal eigenvalue} \(\lambda_1^G\) is the minimum of \(E\) over functions vanishing on \(B\) with \(\sum_xu(x)^2=1\); equivalently, the smallest eigenvalue of the interior block of \(\operatorname{diag}(d)-w-\operatorname{diag}(b)\), where \(d(x)=\sum_yw(x,y)\) is the full degree, boundary edges included. The \emph{interior graph} joins distinct interior vertices of positive weight.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Positive solution on a finite graph; Lean: \texttt{SemioticGraph.exists\_pos\_graph}]\label{thm:graphExistence}
Suppose the interior graph is connected, \(\lambda_1^G<0\), and the \emph{edge condition}
\[
a(x)\le\sqrt{w(x,y)}\quad\text{and}\quad a(y)\le\sqrt{w(x,y)}\qquad\text{for all distinct }x,y\in I\text{ with }w(x,y)>0
\]
holds. Then the graph problem has a solution \(u\) with \(u(x)>0\) for every \(x\in I\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{corollary}[Unweighted graphs; Lean: \texttt{exists\_pos\_graph\_of\_unweighted}]\label{cor:graphUnweighted}
If every weight is \(0\) or \(1\), the edge condition follows from \(0\le a\le 1\), so connectivity of the interior graph and \(\lambda_1^G<0\) suffice.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof route (formalized in \texttt{CdFormal/Graph})]
(i)~\emph{Principal eigendata.} The unit sphere of functions vanishing on \(B\) is compact and, since \(I\ne\emptyset\), nonempty, so \(E\) attains its minimum \(\lambda_1^G\) at some \(\varphi\); the first-order condition gives \(L_G\varphi=b\varphi+\lambda_1^G\varphi\) on \(I\). Because \(E(|\varphi|)\le E(\varphi)\), \(|\varphi|\) is also a minimizer, and a zero of a nonnegative eigenvector at an interior vertex propagates to its neighbours; connectivity of the interior graph then forces \(|\varphi|>0\) on \(I\).
(ii)~\emph{Fixed-point map.} With \(K>0\), the equation at \(x\in I\) is equivalent to \(u(x)=F_K(u)(x)\), where
\[
F_K(u)(x):=\frac{\sum_yw(x,y)u(y)+a(x)|\nabla_Gu|(x)+(b(x)+K)u(x)-c(x)\max\{u(x),0\}^p}{d(x)+K},
\]
and \(F_K(u):=0\) on \(B\); the identity \((d(x)+K)\big(u(x)-F_K(u)(x)\big)=L_Gu(x)-\big(a|\nabla_Gu|+bu-c\,u_+^p\big)(x)\) makes the residual of the equation visible. On functions with values in \([0,M]\) that vanish on \(B\), \(F_K\) is monotone once \(K\) dominates \(a(x)\sum_y\sqrt{w(x,y)}+c(x)pM^{p-1}-b(x)\): raising \(u\) by \(\delta\) at a neighbour \(y\) of \(x\) lowers \(a(x)|\nabla_Gu|(x)\) by at most \(a(x)\sqrt{w(x,y)}\,\delta\le w(x,y)\delta\), which the term \(w(x,y)u(y)\) recovers---this is where the edge condition is used, and the only place.
(iii)~\emph{Barriers.} \(\varepsilon\varphi\) is a subsolution once \(c(x)\varepsilon^{p-1}\le-\lambda_1^G\) for all \(x\); the \emph{plateau} equal to \(M\) on \(I\) and \(0\) on \(B\) is a supersolution once \(a(x)^2/4+b(x)\le c(x)M^{p-1}\) for all \(x\). The plateau is not constant: next to the boundary its Laplacian is \(S=M\sum_{y\in B}w(x,y)\) and its gradient norm is \(\sqrt{MS}\), and the completed square \(a\sqrt{MS}\le S+\tfrac{a^2}{4}M\) absorbs the gradient term.
(iv)~\emph{Fixed point.} \(F_K\) maps the order interval between \(\varepsilon\varphi\) and the plateau, a complete lattice, into itself, so the Knaster--Tarski consequence \texttt{monotone\_fixed\_point\_between} yields a fixed point there; it solves the problem and is at least \(\varepsilon\varphi>0\) on \(I\). All constants are constructed from the data.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Existence versus iteration]\label{rem:graphIteration}
In finite dimensions the monotone iterates \(F_K^n(\varepsilon\varphi)\) are nondecreasing and bounded by the plateau, hence convergent, and by continuity of \(F_K\) their limit is a fixed point. This classical remark justifies the iteration used in the numerical companion, but no convergence rate is claimed, and the Lean development proves existence only. A run of the iteration is validated by its residual, never by the theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[The triangle; Lean: \texttt{SemioticGraph.triangle}]\label{ex:triangle}
Let \(V=\{0,1,2\}\) with \(w\equiv 1\) (including self weights), \(B=\{0\}\), \(\kappa=\gamma=\mu=1\) (so \(a=1\)), \(b=2\), \(c=1\), \(p=2\). The interior graph is the single edge \(\{1,2\}\), the weights lie in \(\{0,1\}\), and \(E(0,1,1)=-2<0\), so \(\lambda_1^G<0\) and Corollary~\ref{cor:graphUnweighted} applies (\texttt{exists\_pos\_triangle}). Explicitly, the interior block of \(\operatorname{diag}(d)-w-\operatorname{diag}(b)\) is \(\begin{psmallmatrix}0&-1\\-1&0\end{psmallmatrix}\), so \(\lambda_1^G=-1\). The function \(u=(0,2,2)\) solves the problem: at each interior vertex \(L_Gu=2\) and \(|\nabla_Gu|=2\), and \(2=1\cdot 2+2\cdot 2-1\cdot 2^2\) (\texttt{triangle\_isSolution}, \texttt{triangle\_gradNorm}); the gradient term is active. Here \(b=2\) is a free admissible coefficient, not the canonical closure \(\kappa\gamma-\lambda\mu\) with \(\lambda>0\). The constants of the proof for this example are \(\varepsilon=\tfrac14\), \(M=7.75\) and \(K=62.5\); the numerical companion reproduces \((0,2,2)\) from both barriers.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Maximum bound on graphs]\label{prop:graphBound}
Let \(u\ge 0\) solve the graph problem and let \(M:=\max_Vu>0\). Then
\[
M\le\max_{x\in I}\Big(\frac{\big(b(x)+a(x)^2/4\big)_+}{c(x)}\Big)^{1/(p-1)} .
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The maximum is attained at some \(x\in I\) since \(u=0\) on \(B\). Put \(S:=L_Gu(x)=\sum_yw(x,y)(M-u(y))\ge 0\). Since \(0\le M-u(y)\le M\),
\[
|\nabla_Gu|(x)^2=\sum_yw(x,y)(M-u(y))^2\le M\sum_yw(x,y)(M-u(y))=MS .
\]
The equation at \(x\) and the inequality \(a\sqrt{MS}\le S+\tfrac{a^2}{4}M\) (the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality; Lean: \texttt{mul\_sqrt\_mul\_le}) give
\[
S=a(x)|\nabla_Gu|(x)+b(x)M-c(x)M^p\le S+\tfrac{a(x)^2}{4}M+b(x)M-c(x)M^p,
\]
hence \(c(x)M^{p-1}\le b(x)+a(x)^2/4\), which implies the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The graph bound differs from the continuum bound]\label{rem:graphBoundSharp}
The continuum bound \((B/c_0)^{1/(p-1)}\) of Lemma~\ref{lem:LinftyBound} uses \(\nabla u=0\) at an interior maximum, which has no graph analogue. The graph bound is attained: with \(V=\{0,1\}\), \(B=\{0\}\), \(w(0,1)=\tfrac14\), \(a=b=c=1\), \(p=2\), the function \(u=(0,\tfrac54)\) solves the graph problem (\(L_Gu(1)=\tfrac5{16}\), \(|\nabla_Gu|(1)=\tfrac58\), and \(\tfrac5{16}=\tfrac58+\tfrac54-\tfrac{25}{16}\)), exceeds the continuum value \(b/c=1\), and equals the graph bound \(1+\tfrac14\). Proposition~\ref{prop:graphBound} is proved here classically; it is not a declaration of the Lean development at the pinned revision, although its key inequality is.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Negativity of \(\lambda_1^G\) is not necessary]\label{prop:graphConverse}
Take the triangle of Example~\ref{ex:triangle} but with \(b=\tfrac12\). Then \(u=(0,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)\) solves the graph problem and is positive at both interior vertices, while \(\lambda_1^G=+\tfrac12\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At each interior vertex \(L_Gu=\tfrac12\), \(|\nabla_Gu|=\tfrac12\), and \(\tfrac12=1\cdot\tfrac12+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12-(\tfrac12)^2\). The interior block of \(\operatorname{diag}(d)-w-\operatorname{diag}(b)\) is \(\begin{psmallmatrix}3/2&-1\\-1&3/2\end{psmallmatrix}\), with smallest eigenvalue \(\tfrac12\).
\end{proof}
This does not contradict Theorem~\ref{thm:graphExistence}, whose spectral hypothesis is sufficient; it shows that the converse fails once the gradient term is active, in line with Remark~\ref{rem:converseGradient}.

\begin{remark}[Three models that must not be identified]\label{rem:threeModels}
The paper now involves three distinct objects: the continuum problem \eqref{eq:V1prime}; the finite-graph problem of Definition~\ref{def:graphModel}, whose theorem is machine-checked; and the centered finite-difference discretization used by the numerical companion, whose gradient in one dimension is \(|(u_{i+1}-u_{i-1})/(2h)|\). The last two differ: on a unit-spaced path with values \((0,1,0)\) the centered derivative at the middle vertex is \(0\) while \(|\nabla_Gu|=\sqrt2\); and on a nearest-neighbour grid with weights \(h^{-2}\), \(|\nabla_Gu|^2=h^{-2}\big((u_{i+1}-u_i)^2+(u_{i-1}-u_i)^2\big)\) has leading term \(2(u')^2\) at smooth nonstationary points, not \((u')^2\). This local expansion is not a convergence theorem, and rescaling the coefficients or normalizing the weights would change the model that was verified. Consequently the finite-difference computations are numerical evidence for the continuum problem, the graph computations reproduce the verified finite model, and neither is a verification of the other.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Temporal resilience and coherence debt: unpacking contradiction cost}\label{sec:temporal_resilience_debt}

The canonical viability closure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical_closure_static}
b(x)=\kappa(x)\gamma(x)-\lambda\,\mu(x)
\end{equation}
introduces a parameter $\lambda>0$ weighting contradiction as a reduction in viability.
In the present section we clarify the two distinct ``lambdas'' introduced in Remark~\ref{rem:lambdaCost} and then propose a temporal refinement of the closure in which contradiction cost is no longer an undetermined constant but an \emph{effective} quantity determined by temporal coherence dynamics.

\paragraph{Two lambdas.}
We distinguish:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\lambda_1(\mathcal{L})$, the \emph{principal Dirichlet eigenvalue} of a linear operator $\mathcal{L}$ (e.g.\ $\mathcal{L}=-\Delta-b$). In the saturated CD model, the spectral sign condition $\lambda_1(-\Delta-b;M)<0$ is a viability indicator: more negative values correspond to stronger distance from the threshold $\lambda_1=0$.
\item $\lambda$ in \eqref{eq:canonical_closure_static}, a \emph{closure parameter} multiplying contradiction $\mu$. In \eqref{eq:canonical_closure_static}, increasing $\lambda$ decreases $b$ for fixed $\mu>0$ and thus makes spectral viability harder to satisfy. Consequently, $\lambda$ functions as a \emph{contradiction cost/sensitivity parameter} (high $\lambda$ = contradiction costly/fragile; low $\lambda$ = contradiction less costly/resilient).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{Temporal witness function and resilience factor}
Let $\psi(t)\in[0,1]$ denote a \emph{temporal witness function} measuring instantaneous coherence at time $t$, with higher values indicating greater coherence and temporal integration. Following a temporal-coherence framework developed in companion work, we interpret derivatives as dynamical signatures:
$\psi'(t)$ (volatility), $\psi''(t)$ (stabilizing vs destabilizing acceleration), and $\psi'''(t)$ (jerk/rupture).

\begin{definition}[Base temporal resilience factor]\label{def:Rbase}
Fix constants $\alpha_1,\alpha_3>0$ and $\beta\ge 0$. Define the \emph{base temporal resilience factor}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rbase}
R_{\mathrm{base}}(t)
:=
\frac{1+\beta[\psi''(t)]^{+}}{1+\alpha_1|\psi'(t)|+\alpha_3|\psi'''(t)|},
\qquad [u]^+:=\max\{u,0\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation]
The numerator rewards ``acceleration toward coherence'' (as encoded by $\psi''>0$ in the $\psi\in[0,1]$ convention), while the denominator penalizes volatility and jerk. Thus $R_{\mathrm{base}}$ is large when coherence dynamics are smooth and stabilizing, and small when coherence changes rapidly or discontinuously.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{Spectral capacity witness and the capacity envelope}
A key architectural principle is that the system has a \emph{sustainable capacity} to maintain coherent presence given its current geometry/boundary conditions and coefficient fields. We encode this capacity spectrally.

Let $M(t)$ denote the effective semiotic manifold at time $t$, allowing for the possibility that the operative system boundary/blanket changes with environment (e.g.\ infrastructure, community, housing). In practice, this can be represented equivalently by changes in effective boundary conditions, domain, or coefficient fields; we do not assume these are separable contributions.

\begin{definition}[Spectral capacity witness]\label{def:psispectral}
Let $b(\cdot,t)$ denote the viability potential at time $t$ and define the principal eigenvalue
\[
\lambda_1(t):=\lambda_1\!\big(-\Delta - b(\cdot,t);\; M(t)\big),
\]
with Dirichlet boundary conditions on $\partial M(t)$.
Fix a scale parameter $s>0$ and define the \emph{spectral capacity witness}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psispectral}
\psi_s(t):=\frac{1}{1+\exp(\lambda_1(t)/s)}\in(0,1).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Meaning of $\psi_s$]
The mapping \eqref{eq:psispectral} converts the real-valued spectral margin $\lambda_1(t)$ into a bounded witness comparable to $\psi(t)$:
more negative $\lambda_1$ (deeper viability) yields $\psi_s$ closer to $1$, while $\lambda_1$ near or above $0$ yields $\psi_s$ near $1/2$ or below.
Other monotone maps $\mathbb{R}\to(0,1)$ are possible; \eqref{eq:psispectral} is chosen for smoothness.
\end{remark}

We interpret $\psi_s(t)$ as a \emph{capacity envelope}: what the current system can sustain given its current structural conditions. In contrast, $\psi(t)$ is an empirical witness of what the system is actually doing.

\subsubsection*{Coherence debt: chronic operation beyond capacity}
The central burnout/recovery phenomenon motivating this section is the possibility that a system can temporarily \emph{perform coherence beyond sustainable capacity}. Formally, this corresponds to $\psi(t)>\psi_s(t)$. Acute excursions are not necessarily pathological; the pathological regime is \emph{sustained} overcapacity operation. We encode this temporally via a debt variable.

\begin{definition}[Coherence debt dynamics]\label{def:debt}
Fix parameters $\eta,\rho>0$. Define the \emph{coherence debt} $D(t)\ge 0$ as the solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:debtODE}
\dot D(t)=\eta[\psi(t)-\psi_s(t)]^{+}-\rho D(t),
\qquad D(0)\ge 0.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation: asymmetric cost of mismatch]
The positive-part term in \eqref{eq:debtODE} implements a structural asymmetry:
only the regime $\psi>\psi_s$ (performing above capacity) accumulates debt.
The decay term $\rho D$ models recovery/repayment when the system is no longer overextended.
Equation \eqref{eq:debtODE} is equivalent to an exponentially weighted integral of past overcapacity, hence captures the chronicity of burnout (``debt that builds over time'') rather than a pointwise discrepancy.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{From temporal resilience to effective contradiction cost}
We now interpret the contradiction weight in \eqref{eq:canonical_closure_static} as an effective cost determined by temporal resilience and debt.

\begin{definition}[Total resilience and effective contradiction cost]\label{def:Reff}
Let $\lambda_0>0$ denote baseline contradiction cost. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rtotal}
R(t):=\frac{R_{\mathrm{base}}(t)}{1+D(t)},
\end{equation}
and the \emph{effective contradiction cost}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambdaeff}
\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}(t):=\frac{\lambda_0}{R(t)}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Burnout spiral as feedback]
When $\psi>\psi_s$ persists, debt $D(t)$ increases, reducing $R(t)$ and therefore increasing $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)$.
Through the viability closure below, this reduces $b(\cdot,t)$, which pushes the spectral indicator $\lambda_1(t)$ upward (toward $0$) and lowers $\psi_s(t)$.
Thus sustained overcapacity operation creates a self-reinforcing fragility loop: capacity declines as debt accumulates.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{Dynamical viability closure}
We replace the constant contradiction weight in \eqref{eq:canonical_closure_static} by $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)$.

\begin{definition}[Dynamical viability closure]\label{def:dynamical_closure}
Define the viability potential as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamical_closure}
b(x,t):=\kappa(x,t)\gamma(x,t)-\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)\,\mu(x,t),
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is given by \eqref{eq:lambdaeff}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Architectural interpretation]
The dynamical closure \eqref{eq:dynamical_closure} makes explicit that contradiction cost is not a free constant but a compressed representation of temporal stability dynamics:
\begin{itemize}
\item smooth, stabilizing coherence trajectories (large $R_{\mathrm{base}}$) reduce effective contradiction cost,
\item chronic overcapacity operation (large $D$) increases effective contradiction cost,
\item changes in the effective system boundary/blanket (encoded by $M(t)$ and the fields on it) shift the capacity envelope $\psi_s$ through $\lambda_1(t)$.
\end{itemize}
This provides a principled bridge between temporal coherence (as measured by $\psi$) and viability in the CD PDE model.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{Capacity erosion (deferred extension)}
The model above treats debt $D$ as a penalty on resilience but does not allow debt to permanently degrade the structural fields that generate capacity. In many real systems, prolonged overcapacity operation can erode care/coherence or increase contradiction exposure (``burning the furniture to heat the house'').

\begin{remark}[Capacity erosion as a future-work extension]\label{rem:capacity_erosion}
A natural extension is to let prolonged debt degrade the characteristic fields or effective system definition. For example, one may posit thresholded degradation laws such as
\[
\dot\kappa = -\delta_\kappa [D-D_{\mathrm{crit}}]^+,\qquad
\dot\gamma = -\delta_\gamma [D-D_{\mathrm{crit}}]^+,\qquad
\dot\mu = +\delta_\mu [D-D_{\mathrm{crit}}]^+,
\]
or alternatively encode erosion by time-dependent boundary/geometry changes in $M(t)$.
We do not analyze such extended dynamics here; the present contribution is the minimal debt-resilience closure \eqref{eq:debtODE}--\eqref{eq:dynamical_closure}.
\end{remark}

\section{Interpretive/structural layer}
This section is conditional by design: it collects definitions and geometric statements that connect the PDE layer to the broader Creative Determinant framework.

\subsection{Weak vs.\ strong coherence (logical status)}
\begin{definition}[Pointwise CD constraint (strong coherence)]
A \emph{strong coherent configuration} is a field \(\Phi\) satisfying
\[
\Phi(x) = \abs{\nabla \Phi(x)}\,\kappa(x)\gamma(x)\mu(x)\qquad\text{for all }x\in M.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Weak coherence]
A \emph{weak coherent configuration} is a solution \(\Phi\) of an associated PDE model (e.g.\ \eqref{eq:V1prime}) with stated boundary conditions.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The generative gap]\label{rem:generativeGap}
The relationship between weak and strong coherence is not merely an open mathematical problem---it is a \emph{structural feature} of the framework. Strong coherence represents an idealization that real systems approximate but cannot fully achieve. Weak coherence captures the stable configurations that systems actually reach. The \emph{distance} between them measures the cost of striving---the energy required to pull a system from its achievable state toward the ideal.

This perspective transforms an apparent limitation into a research program: Under what conditions is the gap small? When is it irreducible? How does the gap evolve under perturbation? The framework is defined by this necessary tension between the ideal and the messy achievable configurations---a commitment that aligns with enactivist insights about cognition as ongoing sense-making rather than static representation~\cite{Thompson2007}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Conditional conservation of blessing--contradiction coupling}
\begin{definition}[Blessing--contradiction 1-form]
Let \(\Phi,\mu\in C^1(M)\). Define the 1-form
\[
\omega := \Phi\,d\mu - \mu\,d\Phi.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional conservation under equilibrium ansatz]\label{prop:conservation}
Let \(U\subseteq M\) be open, and assume an \emph{equilibrium ansatz} on \(U\): there exists a \(C^1\) function \(g:[0,1]\to\R\) such that
\[
\Phi(x)=g(\mu(x))\qquad\text{for all }x\in U.
\]
Then \(\omega\) is closed on \(U\), i.e.\ \(d\omega=0\) on \(U\).
Consequently, for any two homotopic loops \(\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2\subset U\),
\[
\int_{\Gamma_1}\omega = \int_{\Gamma_2}\omega.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Compute
\[
d\omega = d(\Phi\,d\mu)-d(\mu\,d\Phi)=d\Phi\wedge d\mu - d\mu\wedge d\Phi = 2\,d\Phi\wedge d\mu.
\]
Under the ansatz \(\Phi=g(\mu)\), we have \(d\Phi=g'(\mu)\,d\mu\), hence \(d\Phi\wedge d\mu=0\) and thus \(d\omega=0\) on \(U\).
Homotopy invariance follows by Stokes' theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation]
The ansatz \(\Phi=g(\mu)\) expresses a local functional dependence of presence on contradiction, i.e.\ a phase where the presence field is slaved to contradiction level sets.
This is a strong idealization and is stated explicitly as such.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Care-weighted homotopy/energy principle (geometric interpretation)}
\begin{definition}[Care-weighted metric]
On the open set \(\{\kappa>0\}\subseteq M\), define the conformally rescaled metric
\[
g_\kappa := \kappa^{-1} g.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Care-weighted homotopy energy]
Let \(H:S^1\times[0,1]\to M\) be a homotopy such that \(H(S^1\times[0,1])\subset \{\kappa>0\}\).
Define the energy
\[
E_\kappa[H] := \int_0^1\int_{S^1} \left\|\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}\right\|_{g_\kappa}^2\,ds\,dt.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Care as a sufficient obstruction to resolution]\label{prop:careObstruction}
Let \(\gamma_0:S^1\to M\) be a loop. If every homotopy contracting \(\gamma_0\) to a point must intersect the zero-care set \(\{\kappa=0\}\), then there is no contracting homotopy \(H\) with finite \(E_\kappa[H]\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On \(\{\kappa=0\}\), the metric \(g_\kappa=\kappa^{-1}g\) is undefined (formally infinite). Any homotopy forced to pass through \(\{\kappa=0\}\) cannot be assigned finite \(g_\kappa\)-energy.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Status]
This is a geometric sufficient obstruction statement, not an ``iff'' characterization: we do not claim that finite-energy homotopy exists whenever a loop is null-homotopic in \(\{\kappa>0\}\), since that requires additional geometric hypotheses.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The strong ideal is unattainable by nonnegative fields}\label{sec:strongObstruction}
\begin{proposition}[Obstruction to strong coherence]\label{prop:strongObstruction}
Let \(M\) be compact, \(\Phi\in C^1(\overline M)\) with \(\Phi\ge 0\), and assume either \(\partial M\ne\emptyset\) and \(\Phi|_{\partial M}=0\), or \(\partial M=\emptyset\). If \(\Phi(x)=|\nabla\Phi(x)|\,\kappa(x)\gamma(x)\mu(x)\) for all \(x\in M\) with \(\kappa\gamma\mu\) bounded, then \(\Phi\equiv 0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If \(\Phi\not\equiv 0\), then \(\Phi\) attains a positive maximum at some \(x_0\); \(x_0\) is an interior point (in the first case because \(\Phi=0\) on \(\partial M\), in the second because there is no boundary), so \(\nabla\Phi(x_0)=0\) and the constraint gives \(\Phi(x_0)=0\), a contradiction.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Interpretation]\label{rem:strongObstructionInterp}
On a bounded semiotic manifold with dissipative boundary, or on a closed one, the strong ideal cannot be realized by any nonnegative \(C^1\) presence field: strong coherence is not merely unreached but unreachable in this setting, and only weak coherent configurations are attainable. This sharpens the generative gap of Remark~\ref{rem:generativeGap}: the ``work of living'' is not a finite distance to a reachable ideal but an irreducible tension. The proposition uses compactness, the sign of \(\Phi\), the \(C^1\) regularity and the vanishing boundary data; it does not extend by itself to noncompact, nonsmooth, sign-changing or driven settings, where the ideal may remain a meaningful limit.
\end{remark}

\section{Operational layer}
This section proposes testable conditions connecting the CD formalism to measurable dynamical systems.

\subsection{The Creative Determinant condition}
\begin{definition}[Creative Determinant condition \(CD(\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta)\)]
Let \(F:X\to X\) be a \(C^1\) dynamical system on a smooth manifold \(X\), and let \(\varphi:X\to\R_{>0}\) be a pre-specified coherence observable.
We say \((F,\varphi)\) satisfies \(CD(\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta)\) if
\[
\mathrm{Corr}\!\left[\log \varphi(F(x)),\ \alpha\log\abs{\det \nabla F(x)}\right]\ge 1-\varepsilon
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathrm{Var}\!\left[\log\abs{\det \nabla F(x)}\right]>\delta,
\]
where correlation/variance are computed empirically along trajectories or ensembles.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Connection to Lyapunov theory]
By Oseledets' multiplicative ergodic theorem~\cite{Oseledets1968}, time averages of \(\log\abs{\det \nabla F}\) relate to sums of Lyapunov exponents~\cite{Lyapunov1892,Pesin1977}. The CD condition is intended to detect regimes where a coherence observable tracks cumulative phase-space volume deformation. When the CD condition holds with \(\alpha\approx 1\), the correlation suggests the system may exhibit fractal attractor structure, with dimension estimable via the Kaplan--Yorke formula~\cite{KaplanYorke1979}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Falsifiability protocols (empirical proposals)}
We list falsifiability criteria as operational proposals; they are not derived from the PDE theorem.

\begin{definition}[Framework falsification (empirical)]
The CD framework is empirically falsified if any of the following are demonstrated:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(F\arabic*),leftmargin=2.5em]
\item Coherence without coupling: stable high coherence with near-zero (or negative) correlation between coherence observables and Jacobian volume dynamics.
\item Conservation violation: statistically significant disagreement of \(\int_\Gamma\omega\) between homotopic paths within a verified equilibrium region.
\item Hamiltonian coherence: volume-preserving dynamics exhibiting the same coherence--exploration coupling as dissipative systems.
\item Resolution without care: contradiction resolution along paths confined to low-/zero-care regions (as operationalized).
\item Universal uniqueness: systematic absence of multiplicity where the heuristic non-uniqueness picture predicts multiple coherent configurations.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Protocols]
Concrete testing protocols for large language models and other high-dimensional systems can be specified by choosing:
(i) a coherence observable \(\varphi\) (e.g.\ compressibility/entropy/intrinsic dimension proxies),
(ii) a feasible estimator for \(\log\abs{\det \nabla F}\) (e.g.\ factorization across steps, trace estimators, or low-rank sketches),
and (iii) statistical thresholds for correlation and robustness checks.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection*{Mathematical contributions}
The core mathematical results are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Existence framework (Theorem~\ref{thm:existenceV1prime}):} The autonomous viability model with saturation \eqref{eq:V1prime} has a compact fixed-point formulation with a priori bounds for nonnegative weak coherent configurations; the zero field is always a solution, so this theorem alone does not produce presence.
\item \textbf{Positive existence (Theorem~\ref{thm:nontrivialV1prime}):} When the viability threshold is exceeded (\(\lambda_1(-\Delta - b; M) < 0\)), there exists a solution positive in the interior, enclosed between \(\varepsilon\phi_1\) and a constant. This gives a sufficient criterion for when coherent presence can be self-maintained, and by Proposition~\ref{prop:exactThreshold} an exact one when \(a\equiv 0\).
\item \textbf{Finite-graph theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:graphExistence}):} The analogous statement on finite weighted graphs, proved without analytic hypotheses and machine-checked, together with a maximum bound and a counterexample showing that the spectral condition is not necessary once the gradient term is active.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Mathematical limitations}
The saturation term \(-c\Phi^p\) is necessary to break positive homogeneity and enable nontrivial Dirichlet solutions. Without it (the V1 limiting case with \(a\equiv 0\)) a nonnegative nontrivial solution exists only when \(\lambda_1(-\Delta-b;M)=0\), and then only as a multiple of \(\phi_1\); generically only the trivial solution remains. This is a structural constraint, not a proof gap. Three questions are open: uniqueness of the positive solution (Remark~\ref{rem:uniquenessStatus}), whether a positive solution can exist with \(\lambda_1\ge 0\) when \(a>0\) in the continuum (Remark~\ref{rem:converseGradient}; the graph analogue is settled negatively), and the dynamical stability of equilibria, which requires an evolution law not specified here. The continuum theorems are classical proofs; their Lean counterparts are conditional on an analytic interface that the concrete operator does not instantiate (Appendix~\ref{app:verification}).

\subsection*{Conceptual limitations}
The pointwise CD constraint and the PDE proxy are not proven equivalent; this gap is treated as a structural feature (the ``generative tension'') rather than a deficiency. The canonical viability closure \(b = \kappa\gamma - \lambda\mu\) is a modeling choice grounded in observed support-versus-cost dynamics; alternative closures remain open for investigation.

\subsection*{Validating the linear closure}
The canonical closure \(b = \kappa\gamma - \lambda\mu\) is a modeling choice, but one that can be empirically tested. The accompanying computational notebooks (see \texttt{cd\_pde\_demo.ipynb}) include parameter sweeps showing how the bifurcation structure---the ``cliff edge'' where systems transition from nontrivial to trivial equilibria---depends on the closure form.

Future work should:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compare linear, multiplicative (\(b = \kappa\gamma/(1 + \lambda\mu)\)), and exponential (\(b = \kappa\gamma e^{-\lambda\mu}\)) closures.
\item Identify which closure best captures observed bifurcation points in real systems.
\item Use computation to validate the conceptual simplicity of the linear form.
\end{enumerate}

The linear closure is not the final word---it is the \emph{simplest hypothesis} against which alternatives must be tested.

\subsection*{Operational limitations}
The CD condition and falsifiability protocols require careful pre-registration of observables and estimators to avoid tautological fitting; they are empirical proposals.

\subsection*{Relationship to existing frameworks}
The CD framework shares conceptual DNA with several existing formalizations of self-organization:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Autopoiesis}~\cite{MaturanaVarela1980}: CD's ``viability threshold'' operationalizes the autopoietic notion of self-maintenance through boundary conditions.
\item \textbf{Free Energy Principle}~\cite{Friston2010}: Both frameworks formalize how systems maintain coherence against environmental perturbation; CD emphasizes geometric/topological structure while FEP emphasizes information-theoretic bounds.
\item \textbf{Enactivism}~\cite{Varela1991,Thompson2007}: The ``semiotic manifold'' reflects enactivism's emphasis on meaning as emergent from organism-environment coupling.
\item \textbf{Dissipative structures}~\cite{Prigogine1984}: The viability threshold condition is analogous to criticality conditions for far-from-equilibrium self-organization.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Collisions.} The interface is not instantiated by the concrete operators of this paper, for three independent reasons recorded here so that the conditional theorems are not misread. (i)~The field \texttt{T} of \texttt{SolutionOperator} acts on all of \(M\to\R\), and \texttt{T\_compact} uses the product topology; the concrete \(T\) of Section~\ref{sec:mainPDE} is defined and compact only on a \(C^1\) space. (ii)~The default equation is asserted at boundary points too, whereas \eqref{eq:V1prime} imposes the equation in the interior with a separate boundary condition. (iii)~The untruncated operator satisfies neither \texttt{linfty\_bound} nor \texttt{fixed\_point\_nonneg} (Section~\ref{sec:existence}, \emph{What changed}). The Lean implications are correct conditional statements; the discharge of their hypotheses for the concrete problem is the classical analysis of Section~\ref{sec:mainPDE}, which is not formalized.
